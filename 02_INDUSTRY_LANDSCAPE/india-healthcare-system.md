# India Healthcare System Context

## Structure (FACT)
India's healthcare system is a mixed public-private model:
- **Public sector**: primary health centres, district hospitals, and medical colleges, increasingly linked to **Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY)**, a hospitalization-focused insurance scheme covering an estimated **~55 crore beneficiaries** (bottom ~40–50% of the population by income) with up to ₹5 lakh/family/year cover for empanelled secondary/tertiary care.
- **Private sector**: delivers the majority of outpatient care and a large share of inpatient care, financed mostly out-of-pocket or through private insurance, which remains a minority of the population.
- **PMBJP / Jan Aushadhi**: a generic-medicine retail network (18,000+ Kendras as of 2026, ~2,110 medicines including anti-diabetic generics) aimed at affordability, run through the Department of Pharmaceuticals.

## Relevance to Insulin (DBI)
- AB PM-JAY is structurally **hospitalization-oriented**; chronic, outpatient, self-administered therapies like daily insulin are **not its primary design point**, which is a structural gap for a chronic-disease drug category (see `07_MARKET_ACCESS/`).
- PMBJP stocks anti-diabetic generics and is a growing affordability channel, but its assortment and consistency for insulin specifically (versus oral agents) varies, and it primarily supports human insulin/low-cost formulations rather than branded analogues.
- Out-of-pocket expenditure remains the dominant financing mode for most chronic outpatient medicine in India, which is the core reason price sensitivity (rather than clinical preference) drives most volume decisions for insulin (see `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/`).

## Physician and Prescribing Landscape (CA)
- Diabetes is managed by a mix of endocrinologists (concentrated in metro/private care), general physicians, and increasingly diabetologists in Tier 2 cities; **CONSULTING ASSUMPTION**: the majority of insulin initiation in Tier 2/3 India happens through general/internal medicine physicians rather than specialists, making physician education and simplified initiation protocols commercially important, not just specialist detailing.

## Strategic Implication
A market-access strategy for insulin in India must work *around* the hospitalization-centric design of AB PM-JAY (e.g., via state government tie-ups, PMBJP-style channels, employer/insurer outpatient riders, or manufacturer patient-support programs) rather than assume PM-JAY will finance chronic outpatient insulin use at scale (see `07_MARKET_ACCESS/government-programs.md`).
