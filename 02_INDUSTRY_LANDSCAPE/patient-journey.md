# Patient Journey (Insulin-Requiring Diabetes, India)

## Journey Stages (CA — synthesized from clinical/health-systems literature)

1. **At-risk / undiagnosed** — largest pool: up to ~50%+ of people with diabetes in India are estimated to be undiagnosed (multiple sources, incl. PharmEasy analysis of ICMR-INDIAB-17). No insulin relevance yet, but the single largest future-conversion pool.
2. **Diagnosed, oral-agent managed** — the majority of newly diagnosed Type 2 patients start on lifestyle change + metformin/other oral agents.
3. **Oral-agent failure / intensification trigger** — over years, beta-cell decline or acute illness (infection, surgery, pregnancy) triggers a need for insulin; this is the critical **insulin-initiation decision point**, frequently delayed in India due to patient reluctance (fear of injections, perceived "last resort" stigma), cost concern, and physician inertia.
4. **Insulin initiation** — typically starts with basal insulin (human NPH or analogue) added to oral therapy, or premix insulin in simplified-regimen settings.
5. **Titration / regimen intensification** — dose and regimen complexity increase over time (basal-bolus, multiple daily injections) as beta-cell function continues to decline.
6. **Ongoing therapy / persistence** — the highest-risk stage for drop-off: affordability, injection burden, and lack of structured follow-up drive non-persistence, which in turn drives complications.
7. **Complications management** — poorly controlled patients progress to retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy, and cardiovascular disease, at which point costs shift heavily toward complication management rather than insulin itself.
8. **Type 1 diabetes track** (distinct, smaller population) — insulin-dependent from diagnosis, typically diagnosed in childhood/young adulthood; journey is defined by lifelong multiple-daily-injection or pump therapy and is disproportionately represented in patient-assistance/NGO program design (e.g., pediatric Type 1 access programs) because these patients cannot survive without continuous insulin access.

## Key Drop-off Points (DBI)
- **Stage 3→4 (initiation delay)**: physician and patient inertia is well documented in Indian diabetology literature as a major driver of late insulin initiation, worsening long-term outcomes.
- **Stage 6 (persistence)**: affordability and injection burden are the dominant reasons for treatment interruption identified in adherence literature (see `03_MARKET_ANALYSIS/adoption-barriers.md`).

## Strategic Implication
The commercial and public-health opportunity is concentrated at two points: (a) reducing initiation delay through physician education and simplified regimens, and (b) improving persistence through affordability programs and patient support — both are addressed in `11_STRATEGIC_OPTIONS/option-D-ecosystem-strategy.md`.
