# Diabetes Landscape in India

## Prevalence (FACT)
- The ICMR-INDIAB study (Anjana et al., *Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology*, 2023 — the largest nationally representative NCD survey in India, ~113,000 individuals across all states/UTs) found a weighted diabetes prevalence of **11.4%** among adults, translating to a government-cited estimate of **10.1 crore (≈101 million) people with diabetes** in India (PIB, 2023), plus an estimated **136 million with prediabetes** — a large future-conversion pool.
- India is the country with the second-highest absolute diabetes burden globally, behind China, and IDF/press estimates place India's share of the global diabetes population at roughly one-quarter.
- Type 2 diabetes accounts for ~90–95% of diagnosed cases in India; Type 1 is comparatively rare but clinically insulin-dependent from diagnosis.

## Diagnosis and Control Gaps (FACT / DBI)
- Awareness and control are weak: national survey data (NNMS, cited in peer-reviewed LMIC health-systems literature) show that among people with diabetes, roughly 46% were aware of their diagnosis, ~36% were on treatment, and only ~16% had blood glucose in control.
- **DATA-BASED INFERENCE**: This "diagnosis → treatment → control" cascade, not raw prevalence, is the real determinant of the insulin-eligible population — a large share of the diagnosed pool is undertreated rather than optimally treated, implying the near-term commercial opportunity is as much about treatment intensification as new-patient acquisition.

## Regional and Socioeconomic Pattern (FACT)
- Historically an urban, higher-income disease, diabetes prevalence in India has converged toward rural areas, and the highest-risk states include Kerala, Delhi, Haryana, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh (Scientific Reports, 2025, using ICMR-INDIAB data) — implying insulin demand is geographically broad, not limited to metro private care.

## Trajectory (SA)
- Projections associated with the ICMR-INDIAB program and IDF Diabetes Atlas point to continued growth in absolute diabetes prevalence over the next decade, driven by urbanization, dietary transition, an aging population, and improved diagnosis infrastructure. This report's model (`10_FINANCIAL_MODEL/`) uses a **6–8% base-case annual growth rate in the insulin-treated population** — higher than raw prevalence growth because of rising diagnosis and earlier treatment initiation, moderated by GLP-1 substitution among higher-income Type 2 patients.

## Strategic Implication
The addressable disease burden is large and still under-diagnosed and under-treated — the constraint on insulin demand is less "not enough patients" and more "not enough of the existing patient pool reaching appropriate, sustained insulin therapy." This reframes the commercial problem from market creation to market activation and retention (see `03_MARKET_ANALYSIS/adoption-barriers.md`).
