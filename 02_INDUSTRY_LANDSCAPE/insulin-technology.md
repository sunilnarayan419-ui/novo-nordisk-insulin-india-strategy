# Insulin Technology & Biology

## How Insulin Works (FACT — foundational biology)
Insulin is a 51-amino-acid peptide hormone secreted by pancreatic beta cells (islets of Langerhans) in response to rising blood glucose. It binds the insulin receptor on muscle, liver, and adipose cells, triggering GLUT4 transporter translocation to the cell membrane and glucose uptake, while suppressing hepatic glucose output. In diabetes, this system fails either because beta cells are destroyed (Type 1, autoimmune) or because tissue insulin resistance combined with progressive beta-cell dysfunction overwhelms the body's own insulin supply (Type 2) — in both cases, exogenous insulin restores glucose disposal.

## Manufacturing Technology (FACT)
- Modern insulin (human and analogue) is produced by **recombinant DNA technology**: the human insulin gene (or an engineered analogue sequence) is inserted into a production organism — historically *E. coli* or *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (yeast) — which is fermented at scale, after which the expressed proinsulin/insulin precursor is purified, enzymatically processed (e.g., proinsulin cleavage), and crystallized/formulated.
- **Analogue insulins** are engineered by modifying the native insulin amino-acid sequence (e.g., glargine adds two arginines and substitutes glycine for asparagine; aspart substitutes proline with aspartic acid) to alter self-association/absorption kinetics — achieving flatter (basal) or faster (bolus) pharmacokinetic profiles than native human insulin.
- **Biosimilar insulins** replicate an already-approved reference analogue's amino-acid sequence and clinical profile through a separate, WHO/regulator-reviewed manufacturing and analytical comparability process; India is a significant global base for such biosimilar manufacturing (e.g., Biocon Biologics).

## Cost Structure Implication (FACT/DBI)
- Published pharmacoeconomic analysis of API export pricing (van de Pol et al., 2019, cited via PMC) estimates human recombinant insulin API costs around **US$24,750/kg**, versus roughly **US$68,757/kg for insulin glargine** and up to **~US$100,000/kg** for other analogues — meaning the *manufacturing cost basis* for analogues is inherently higher than human insulin, but far below the multiple implied by current retail price differentials, which is why biosimilar entry has been able to compress analogue prices substantially in some markets.

## Delivery Technology (FACT)
- Delivery formats — vials with syringes, reusable/durable pens with cartridges, disposable prefilled pens — materially affect dosing accuracy, convenience, and adherence, but also cost: pens typically carry a meaningfully higher unit cost than vials for the same insulin, which is itself an access lever (see `05_PRODUCT_AND_PORTFOLIO/convenience-and-adherence.md`).

## Strategic Implication
The underlying manufacturing economics support meaningful analogue price reduction via biosimilar competition and scale, well below current branded pricing — Novo Nordisk's long-term competitive position depends on value-added differentiation (devices, digital adherence tools, formulation improvements) rather than defending a manufacturing-cost-based price premium that biosimilar entrants can erode.
