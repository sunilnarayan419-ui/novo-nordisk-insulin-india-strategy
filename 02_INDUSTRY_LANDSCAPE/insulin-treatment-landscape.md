# Insulin Treatment Landscape

## Treatment Categories (FACT)
| Category | Examples | Typical Role |
|---|---|---|
| Human insulin — short-acting (regular/soluble) | Human Actrapid-type formulations | Mealtime coverage, hospital/acute use |
| Human insulin — intermediate (NPH) | Human Insulatard-type | Basal coverage, cost-sensitive segments |
| Human premix (e.g., 30:70) | Human Mixtard-type | Simplified twice-daily regimens, common first-line insulin in India |
| Analogue — rapid-acting | Aspart, lispro, glulisine | Better postprandial control, lower hypoglycemia risk vs. regular human insulin |
| Analogue — long-acting/basal | Glargine, detemir, degludec | Flatter profile, once-daily dosing, lower nocturnal hypoglycemia risk |
| Analogue premix | Biphasic aspart/lispro mixes | Convenience + improved profile vs. human premix |

## Clinical Positioning (FACT)
- Analogue insulins offer meaningfully lower hypoglycemia risk and more predictable pharmacokinetics than human insulin, which is clinically valuable especially for elderly patients, those with renal impairment, and those on complex regimens.
- Human insulin remains WHO Essential Medicines List–listed and clinically effective for a large share of patients, particularly where hypoglycemia risk is lower and cost sensitivity is high — the clinical gap between human and analogue insulin, while real, is smaller in an average Type 2 patient than the price gap between them.

## India-Specific Utilization Pattern (DBI)
- Human insulin (especially premix) still represents a substantial share of insulin volume in India relative to high-income markets, reflecting cost sensitivity; IQVIA Institute LMIC data notes human insulins account for a majority share of days-of-therapy across the broader LMIC set studied, with India-like markets skewing toward human/premix relative to Western Europe/North America.
- Analogue share is rising but is gated by price, not physician preference — most Indian diabetologists would prefer analogues for eligible patients if cost were not a constraint (consistent with clinical-value literature).
