# Industry Drivers

## Demand-Side Drivers (FACT / DBI)
1. **Rising and under-treated prevalence** — ~101 million diagnosed diabetics (ICMR-INDIAB, 2023), most undertreated, is a structural demand tailwind independent of any one company's actions.
2. **Urbanization and dietary/lifestyle transition** — long-documented driver of Type 2 diabetes incidence across LMICs, India included.
3. **Aging population** — rising median age increases Type 2 and long-duration Type 1 prevalence requiring intensification to insulin.
4. **Improving diagnostic infrastructure** — expansion of primary-care screening (NP-NCD programme) increases the diagnosed base over time, a precursor to insulin initiation.

## Supply-Side / Industry Structure Drivers (FACT)
5. **Global oligopoly, local fragmentation** — Novo Nordisk, Sanofi, and Eli Lilly hold ~90% of the *global* insulin market by value, but in India and other LMICs, domestic and biosimilar manufacturers (Biocon Biologics, Wockhardt, and others) capture a materially larger volume share (IQVIA Institute: ~28% of insulin volume in India held by "other" manufacturers vs. the big three) — a more competitive, price-elastic local market than the global picture suggests.
6. **Biosimilar entry** — biosimilar insulins (glargine, aspart) are commercially established in India (e.g., Biocon's biosimilar insulin franchise, built partly on India-based manufacturing and API cost advantages), continuously pressuring analogue pricing.
7. **India as a low-cost API/manufacturing base** — India is a significant global exporter of insulin API and finished formulations, which structurally supports low-cost biosimilar competition domestically.

## Policy Drivers (FACT)
8. **Price control (DPCO/NPPA)** — human insulin formulations have long been under NLEM/DPCO price control; insulin glargine (a major analogue) was newly added to NLEM in 2022, with an NPPA ceiling price fixed for both vials and pens — signaling continued/expanding government willingness to price-regulate analogues, not just human insulin.
9. **Government affordability programs** — PMBJP/Jan Aushadhi expansion and Ayushman Bharat coverage broadening (e.g., extension to citizens 70+) reflect sustained political priority on medicine affordability and access.

## Strategic Implication
The direction of travel is unambiguous: growing demand, more price regulation, more biosimilar/domestic competition, and continued affordability-program expansion. A strategy that assumes stable premium pricing power or muted local competition is not durable; the winning strategy must be built for a market that is simultaneously growing and structurally price-compressing.
