# Competitive Threats

## Threat 1 — Biosimilar Price Erosion (FACT/DBI)
Biocon Biologics and other domestic manufacturers can offer biosimilar analogues (glargine, aspart) at a fraction of originator pricing, backed by India-based low-cost manufacturing and WHO prequalification credibility. This is the single largest structural threat to Novo Nordisk's analogue revenue per unit in India, and is a trend (not a one-time event) — the biosimilar insulin market globally is growing at a materially faster rate (~8.9% CAGR cited in industry analysis) than the branded segment.

## Threat 2 — GLP-1 Substitution (FACT/SA)
As GLP-1 receptor agonists (and increasingly lower-cost/generic-adjacent versions) become more available in India, a share of Type 2 patients who would previously have progressed to insulin may instead be managed on GLP-1 therapy for longer, delaying or reducing insulin category growth in the higher-income segment where Novo Nordisk's analogue economics are strongest. Novo Nordisk's own global GLP-1 franchise partially hedges this at the corporate level, but the *insulin* business specifically faces this as a genuine threat.

## Threat 3 — Government Price-Control Expansion (FACT)
The 2022 addition of insulin glargine to the National List of Essential Medicines — with an NPPA ceiling price fixed for both vials and disposable pens — demonstrates that Indian regulators are willing to extend price control beyond human insulin into modern analogues. Further NLEM expansion (e.g., to other basal or rapid analogues) would compress margin on Novo Nordisk's highest-value SKUs.

## Threat 4 — Domestic Manufacturer Institutional Relationships
Domestic players often have stronger, longer-standing relationships with state government procurement bodies and public-sector tenders, a structural advantage in the institutional/government channel that is difficult for a multinational to fully replicate.

## Threat 5 — New Entrant / Import Competition
Additional international biosimilar or generic insulin manufacturers (e.g., other Asia-based manufacturers) could enter India, especially if regulatory pathways for biosimilar insulin approval continue to streamline globally.

## Net Threat Assessment
The combined effect of Threats 1–3 is a structural, multi-year margin-compression trend on analogue insulin in India, independent of any specific competitor action — this is the central reason the report does not recommend a "premium-only" strategy (see `11_STRATEGIC_OPTIONS/option-A-premium-strategy.md` for why it scores lower on sustainability).
