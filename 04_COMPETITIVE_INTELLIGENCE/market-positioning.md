# Market Positioning Map

## Two-Axis Positioning (Price vs. Clinical/Innovation Value)

```
High clinical/innovation value
        │
        │        [Novo Nordisk analogues + devices]
        │        [Sanofi glargine franchise]
        │        [Eli Lilly analogues]
        │
        │                    [Emerging: next-gen basal/combo pipeline]
        │
        │   [Biocon biosimilar analogues — rising quality, lower price]
        │
        │   [Wockhardt human insulin/analogues]
        │
        │   [Domestic human insulin — PMBJP-aligned generics]
Low clinical/innovation value
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────────►
        Low price                                    High price
```

## Reading the Map
- Novo Nordisk (and Sanofi, Lilly) occupy the high-value/high-price quadrant, which is defensible in the premium private/insured channel but structurally vulnerable in the government/institutional and rural out-of-pocket channels.
- Biocon Biologics is the key player pulling the "high clinical value, low price" quadrant into existence — the biosimilar analogue at a fraction of originator cost is the most disruptive competitive position in the market and the one most likely to reshape the map over the 5-year horizon.
- Domestic human-insulin manufacturers anchor the low-price/low-innovation quadrant, competing almost entirely on government tenders and PMBJP-aligned generics.

## Positioning Recommendation
Novo Nordisk should defend its high-value quadrant with device and innovation differentiation while deliberately creating a second, distinct position in the "accessible value" space (segmented human-insulin/affordability-program offer) rather than ceding that space entirely to Biocon and domestic players — see `11_STRATEGIC_OPTIONS/option-C-segmented-strategy.md`.
