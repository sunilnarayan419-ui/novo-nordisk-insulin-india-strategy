# Product Comparison

| Attribute | Human Insulin (own + competitor) | Novo Nordisk Analogues | Competitor Analogues (Sanofi/Lilly) | Biosimilar Analogues (Biocon et al.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hypoglycemia risk | Higher (less predictable PK) | Lower | Lower | Lower (bioequivalent to reference) |
| Dosing flexibility | Lower | Higher (flatter/faster profiles) | Higher | Comparable to reference analogue |
| Device options | Vial/basic pen | Full range incl. reusable + disposable pens, dose-memory features | Vial to premium pen | Typically vial + basic pen; device sophistication usually lags originator |
| Price | Lowest (price-controlled) | Highest among analogues (until price control expands) | Similar to Novo Nordisk | 30–60% below originator |
| Regulatory status in India | Long-established, NLEM-listed | Approved, glargine-class now NLEM-listed | Approved | Approved, some WHO-prequalified |
| Government/tender competitiveness | High | Low–moderate | Low–moderate | High |
| Brand/physician trust (India) | Moderate | High | Moderate–high | Growing, still building trust vs. originators in some segments |

## Interpretation
Novo Nordisk's product-level differentiation is real (device quality, PK predictability) but narrowing as biosimilar analogues achieve comparable clinical bioequivalence at substantially lower prices — the sustainable differentiator is shifting from "the molecule/device" to "the total offer" (device + patient support + access program), which is the basis for the ecosystem strategic option.
