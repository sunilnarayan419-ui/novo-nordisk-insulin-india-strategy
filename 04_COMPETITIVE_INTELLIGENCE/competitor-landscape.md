# Competitor Landscape

## Global Originators Active in India (FACT)
- **Novo Nordisk** — full human insulin + analogue portfolio (incl. glargine-class competitor via degludec, aspart, premix analogues), longstanding India manufacturing/commercial presence, global insulin category leader by revenue.
- **Eli Lilly** — human insulin + analogue portfolio (lispro, glargine-class competitor via basaglar-type biosimilar strategies globally), smaller India insulin footprint than in the US/global market, increasingly GLP-1/obesity-focused globally.
- **Sanofi** — long-acting analogue (glargine — Lantus/Toujeo franchise globally), significant historical presence in insulin glargine, facing biosimilar erosion globally including in India.

## Domestic / Biosimilar Players (FACT)
- **Biocon Biologics** — India's largest domestic insulin/biosimilar player; manufactures human insulin and biosimilar insulin glargine and aspart; has WHO-prequalified biosimilar insulin products and a global biosimilar insulin franchise (including US interchangeable biosimilar glargine, Semglee, via Viatris partnership) — a credible low-cost analogue competitor domestically and internationally.
- **Wockhardt** — established Indian human insulin and analogue manufacturer with a long-standing domestic insulin franchise.
- Other domestic/regional manufacturers participate primarily in human insulin and government tender channels, consistent with IQVIA Institute data showing "other" manufacturers hold a notably higher share of India insulin volume (~28%) than in high-income markets.

## Market Structure (FACT)
Globally, Novo Nordisk, Sanofi, and Eli Lilly collectively hold ~90% of the insulin market by *value*; however, in India specifically, the "big three" share of *volume* is materially diluted by strong domestic/biosimilar competition (consistent with IQVIA Institute LMIC data), making India one of the more competitively fragmented major insulin markets globally.

## Strategic Read
India is not a market where Novo Nordisk can rely on global oligopoly dynamics to protect price or share — domestic biosimilar competitors have real manufacturing scale, cost advantages (India is a global low-cost API base), and regulatory credibility (WHO prequalification), meaning competitive strategy must be built around differentiation (device, brand trust, access programs, physician relationships) rather than assuming structural pricing power.
