# Portfolio Gaps

## Identified Gaps (STRATEGIC JUDGMENT)
1. **Explicit affordability-tier SKU/packaging**: while Novo Nordisk's human insulin exists, there is limited evidence of a deliberately branded, actively promoted "affordability tier" comparable to how PMBJP/Jan Aushadhi has built trust around low-cost generics — an explicit affordability sub-brand could better compete for price-sensitive volume without diluting premium brand equity.
2. **Structured patient-support/access program at national scale**: unlike some LMIC markets where Novo Nordisk has run visible "Changing Diabetes"-style access initiatives, India-specific program visibility (co-pay assistance, patient education infrastructure) appears less developed relative to the scale of India's affordability gap.
3. **Digital adherence/monitoring integration**: limited evidence of an India-market-adapted digital companion (dose reminders, simplified glucose-log integration) bundled with the insulin portfolio, which competitors and adjacent GLP-1 players are increasingly using as a differentiator.
4. **Institutional/government-tender-competitive human insulin SKU**: to the extent Novo Nordisk's human insulin cost structure is not fully optimized for aggressive government tender pricing, this cedes institutional-channel volume (and the associated physician/hospital relationship equity) to domestic competitors.

## Non-Gaps (Where Portfolio Breadth Is Already Sufficient)
- Core molecule coverage (human insulin through modern basal/rapid analogues) is comprehensive; the gap is in **market-facing packaging, pricing architecture, and program infrastructure**, not underlying molecule/device R&D.

## Strategic Implication
Closing these gaps is largely a **commercial-model and go-to-market investment**, not a product-development problem — this shapes the investment requirements in `12_IMPLEMENTATION/investment-requirements.md` toward market-access infrastructure rather than R&D.
