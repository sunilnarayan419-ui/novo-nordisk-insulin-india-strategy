# Insulin Product Landscape (Novo Nordisk-Relevant)

## Category Structure
1. **Human insulin** — short-acting (regular/soluble), intermediate (NPH), premix (30:70 and similar ratios) — vials and cartridges.
2. **Rapid-acting analogues** — aspart-class; used for mealtime/bolus coverage with faster onset than regular human insulin.
3. **Long-acting/basal analogues** — including flatter, longer-duration options; used for once-daily basal coverage with lower nocturnal hypoglycemia risk than NPH.
4. **Premix analogues** — combine rapid-acting analogue with a protaminated/intermediate component for simplified twice-daily dosing with an improved profile versus human premix.
5. **Delivery devices** — vials + syringes, durable/reusable pen injectors (cartridge-based), disposable prefilled pens.

## Portfolio Role by Segment
| Product Category | Primary Channel Fit |
|---|---|
| Human insulin/premix | Government/institutional, PMBJP-aligned, rural/Tier 3+ |
| Long-acting analogue | Premium private, specialist-managed, Type 1 and complex Type 2 |
| Rapid-acting analogue | Premium private, intensive/basal-bolus regimens |
| Premix analogue | Mainstream private, Tier 1/2, simplicity-seeking patients willing to pay a moderate premium |
| Disposable pens | Convenience-oriented, higher-income, elderly/low-dexterity patients |

## Portfolio Assessment
Novo Nordisk's India-relevant portfolio spans the full category structure above — the strategic question is not "does the portfolio have gaps" so much as "is the portfolio deployed with the right price/channel architecture to match segment-specific willingness and ability to pay" (see `portfolio-strategy.md` and `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/`).
