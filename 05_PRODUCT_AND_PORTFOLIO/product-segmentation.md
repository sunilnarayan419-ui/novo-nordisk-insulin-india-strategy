# Product-to-Segment Mapping

| Patient Segment | Recommended Primary Product | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Premium private / insured, complex Type 2 | Long-acting + rapid-acting analogue, premium pen | Clinical need for flexibility + PK predictability; ability to pay |
| Mainstream private, newly initiated Type 2 | Premix analogue or premix human insulin (tiered by price sensitivity) | Simplicity for physician and patient; lower behavioral barrier to initiation |
| Government/institutional | Human insulin (vial), tender-priced | Price is the dominant selection criterion in this channel |
| Rural/Tier 3+, out-of-pocket | Human insulin (vial), PMBJP-aligned pricing/packaging | Affordability and distribution-channel fit take priority over device sophistication |
| Type 1 diabetes (all income levels, prioritized regardless of channel) | Basal-bolus analogue regimen, with patient-support co-pay assistance for lower-income patients | Clinical necessity is absolute; patient-support program bridges affordability gap |

## Design Principle
Product-segment fit should be actively managed rather than left to organic channel drift — i.e., Novo Nordisk should deliberately merchandise/price specific SKUs for specific channels (a "good-better-best" architecture) rather than relying on a single national price list that under-serves both the premium and access ends of the market simultaneously.
