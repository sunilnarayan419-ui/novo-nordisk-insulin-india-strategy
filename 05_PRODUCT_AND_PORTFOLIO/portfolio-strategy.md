# Portfolio Strategy

## Recommended Portfolio Architecture: Segmented "Good-Better-Best"
1. **Access tier (human insulin, vial-based, tender/PMBJP-competitive pricing)** — defend institutional and rural/Tier 3+ volume, maintain government relationship equity, and support the "access" narrative that provides political/regulatory cover.
2. **Mainstream tier (premix analogue and standard analogues, moderate pricing, patient-support-program-eligible)** — the volume growth engine, targeted at Tier 1/2 mainstream private patients.
3. **Premium tier (latest-generation basal/rapid analogues, premium devices)** — margin engine, targeted at premium private/insured segment and complex-need patients (Type 1, elderly, renal impairment) regardless of channel.

## Portfolio Allocation Principle
Investment (detailing, marketing, patient-support subsidy) should be allocated to maximize **segment-specific unit economics**, not applied uniformly — meaning the access tier is subsidized by margin from the premium tier as a deliberate cross-subsidy, not treated as a stand-alone profit center (consistent with the access-vs-margin trade-off framework in `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/access-vs-margin-tradeoff.md`).

## Should Novo Nordisk Prioritize Premium, Human Insulin, Affordability, or Segmented? (Direct Answer to KMQ5)
**Recommendation: Segmented portfolio strategy (Option C), evolving toward an ecosystem model (Option D) over the 5-year horizon.** A pure premium strategy cedes the majority price-sensitive market to competitors and shrinks long-term relevance; a pure affordability strategy risks an unwinnable price war against lower-cost domestic manufacturing; a segmented approach captures margin where defensible and volume/access where necessary, while an ecosystem layer (patient support, digital tools, provider training) builds a moat that is harder for biosimilar-only competitors to replicate on price alone.
