# Commercial Risks

## Co1 — Physician/Patient Perception of Segmented Pricing as "Two-Tier" Medicine
**Description**: A segmented portfolio/pricing strategy could be perceived (by physicians, media, or patient advocates) as offering "better medicine to the rich" — a reputational risk if not carefully communicated.
**Likelihood**: Moderate.
**Impact**: Moderate — primarily reputational, with potential knock-on regulatory attention if the narrative gains traction.
**Mitigation**: Frame and communicate the strategy explicitly around **expanding total access** (i.e., the access tier is a net addition serving previously underserved patients, not a downgrade for existing patients) — consistent, proactive external communication is a necessary companion to the internal strategy, not an afterthought.

## Co2 — Sales Force Incentive Misalignment
**Description**: A commercial team historically incentivized on premium-analogue volume/margin may under-invest effort in the mainstream/access tiers central to this strategy's access goals.
**Likelihood**: Moderate-to-high without deliberate change management.
**Impact**: Moderate — could materially slow realized access-tier growth even if the strategy and pricing are correctly designed.
**Mitigation**: Redesign incentive structures to explicitly reward tier-appropriate volume and patient-support program enrollment, not premium-tier revenue alone; management attention to this is a specific implementation workstream, not assumed to happen automatically.

## Co3 — Institutional Partner Concentration Risk
**Description**: Over-reliance on a small number of large institutional/insurer partners for Year 1–2 pilot programs creates concentration risk if any single partnership underperforms or is discontinued.
**Likelihood**: Moderate (inherent in a pilot-first approach).
**Impact**: Low-to-moderate (bounded by the pilot scale itself).
**Mitigation**: Diversify pilot partnerships across at least 2–3 institutions/insurers rather than a single flagship partnership, to avoid single-point-of-failure risk in the evidence-generation process.
