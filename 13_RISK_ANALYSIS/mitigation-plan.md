# Consolidated Mitigation Plan

## Cross-Cutting Mitigation Themes

| Theme | Addresses | Key Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Proactive, not reactive, pricing/regulatory strategy | R1, R2, S2 | Anticipatory pricing scenario planning for next-gen products before launch; sustained visible access investment |
| Differentiation over price competition | C1, C2, C3, F1 | Ecosystem build (device, digital, patient support) as the primary competitive response, not price matching |
| Phased, gated investment | F3, O3, S1 | Year-over-year review gates before committing next-phase capital (see `12_IMPLEMENTATION/implementation-roadmap.md`) |
| Strong internal governance | O2, Co2 | Pricing governance sub-committee, incentive redesign, cross-functional working group (see `12_IMPLEMENTATION/governance-model.md`) |
| Diversified partnerships | Co3, O4 | Multiple institutional/insurer pilots rather than single flagship dependency |
| Proactive external communication | Co1 | Explicit "expanding total access" narrative accompanying the segmented-pricing rollout |
| Continuous monitoring and recalibration | S3, all competitive risks | Annual model recalibration, KPI-based early-warning tracking (see `12_IMPLEMENTATION/KPI-framework.md`) |

## Risk Ownership
Each risk in this section should be assigned a named owner within the governance structure (`12_IMPLEMENTATION/governance-model.md`) responsible for monitoring its associated KPI/leading indicators and triggering escalation per the defined review triggers.

## Residual Risk After Mitigation
Even with full mitigation, the strategy carries genuine residual risk — most significantly R1 (further price control) and C1 (biosimilar share gains) — which is why the financial model's Downside Case is treated as a plausible, not merely theoretical, outcome, and the recommended strategy is explicitly designed to remain directionally sound (continued patient volume growth, defensible though compressed margin) even if the Downside Case materializes.
