# Executive Summary

## The Question
How should Novo Nordisk expand insulin access and capture sustainable growth in India over the next 3–5 years without compromising the long-term economics and strategic positioning of its insulin business?

## The Market
India has an estimated ~101 million people with diagnosed diabetes (ICMR-INDIAB, 2023), the majority undertreated, with an estimated ~9.8 million currently insulin-treated (this report's triangulated estimate). This population is structurally growing (~6–8% annually in the base case) even as government price control (NPPA/DPCO) and domestic/biosimilar competition compress per-unit economics — India's insulin market is not one market but several, with prices ranging from a government-regulated ~₹15/ml for human insulin to reported premium analogue pen prices exceeding ₹7,000, a near-90x spread.

## The Core Finding
Access and margin are only in genuine tension under a uniform national pricing approach. A deliberately **segmented portfolio and pricing architecture** — different price points and product tiers for premium, mainstream, institutional, and rural/access channels — converts most of the apparent trade-off into a better allocation of the same total margin pool, because price elasticity and ability-to-pay vary enormously by segment.

## The Recommendation
Adopt a **segmented portfolio + differentiated access strategy now**, with a funded, phased roadmap to build toward a fuller **ecosystem strategy** (patient support, digital adherence tools, provider training, institutional/insurer partnerships) over the 5-year horizon. This scored highest on a 10-criterion weighted decision framework against three alternative strategic options (premium-only, access/price-led-only, and a full ecosystem build attempted immediately).

## The Numbers
The accompanying quantitative model (`10_FINANCIAL_MODEL/`) projects Base Case Year-5 revenue of **~₹2,083 crore (~US$247M)** at **~63% blended gross margin**, growing to **~₹2,806 crore (~US$332M)** in the Upside Case. Critically, even the Downside Case (further price control, faster biosimilar erosion) shows continued **patient-volume growth** — the primary risk this strategy must manage is margin compression, not market disappearance.

## The Path
Year 1: segmented pricing rollout, PMBJP distribution partnership, stock-out reduction, Type 1 diabetes patient-support program. Year 2: geographic and digital-tool expansion, insurer pilots. Years 3–5: national ecosystem scale, reimbursement-policy advocacy, and a data-driven review of local manufacturing investment.

## The Risk
The most material risks are further NLEM/price-control expansion and accelerated biosimilar share gains — both are explicitly modeled in the Downside Case and addressed through proactive (not reactive) pricing strategy and ecosystem-based differentiation rather than price competition against structurally lower-cost domestic manufacturers.
