# Decision Framework

## Decision to be Made
Select and commit resources to one of four strategic options (or a defined hybrid) for Novo Nordisk's India insulin business, and set the associated pricing/access, portfolio, and operating-model commitments for FY1–FY5.

## Decision Criteria and Weights (illustrative — see `strategic-scorecard.md` for full scoring)

| Criterion | Weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Patient impact / access expansion | 20% | Central to the mandate ("expand access") and to reputational/political license to operate |
| Revenue potential | 15% | Commercial sustainability requirement |
| Margin / portfolio economics | 15% | Must not "destroy portfolio economics" per problem statement |
| Competitive defensibility | 15% | Avoid commoditization exposure |
| Execution complexity / capability fit | 10% | Feasibility within realistic org capacity |
| Investment requirement | 10% | Capital discipline |
| Regulatory/access risk | 10% | Policy environment (price control, compulsory licensing risk) |
| Strategic optionality / sustainability | 5% | Ability to adapt to a fast-moving market (biosimilars, GLP-1 shift) |

## Decision Rule
The recommended option must (a) score highest or near-highest on the weighted scorecard, (b) not require an unfunded or organizationally implausible step-change in investment, and (c) remain viable under the downside scenario in the financial model (`10_FINANCIAL_MODEL/`).

## Escalation / Review Triggers
Post-decision, the strategy should be revisited if: (i) a compulsory-license or drastic new price-control action targets insulin, (ii) a competitor achieves a >5-point share shift in two consecutive years, or (iii) GLP-1 substitution measurably reduces insulin-eligible Type 2 population growth.
