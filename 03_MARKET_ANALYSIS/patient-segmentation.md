# Patient Segmentation

## Clinical Segments
1. **Type 1 diabetes** (~3–4 million, estimate) — insulin-dependent from diagnosis, highest clinical urgency, disproportionately pediatric/young-adult, highest need for continuity of supply and affordability support (missed doses are acutely dangerous).
2. **Type 2, newly insulin-initiated** — largest growth segment; decision point is physician-led; regimen simplicity (premix or basal-only) is the priority.
3. **Type 2, long-duration/intensified** — more complex regimens, higher per-patient insulin volume, more likely to be managed by specialists in Tier 1/metro care.
4. **Gestational diabetes** — insulin used when needed during pregnancy; time-limited but high-urgency segment; important for maternal/child health programs and public-sector engagement even though commercially small.

## Socioeconomic / Payer Segments
| Segment | Approx. share of insulin-treated population (CA) | Payer behavior | Price sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium private (metro, insured/high-income) | ~10–15% | Private pay / insurance | Low–moderate |
| Mainstream private (Tier 1/2 urban, middle income) | ~35–40% | Predominantly out-of-pocket | High |
| Public/institutional (AB PM-JAY hospitals, state programs) | ~20–25% | Government-financed, tender-priced | Very high (price-controlled) |
| Rural / Tier 3+ / low-income | ~25–30% | Out-of-pocket, PMBJP where available | Extremely high; also access-constrained |

## Geographic Segments
- **Metro/Tier 1** — highest analogue penetration, specialist-driven, best supply-chain reliability
- **Tier 2** — largest absolute growth opportunity; general-physician-led initiation; moderate infrastructure
- **Tier 3+/rural** — largest unmet need; cold-chain and last-mile distribution are binding constraints as much as price (see `09_SUPPLY_CHAIN/`)

## Strategic Segmentation Priority (STRATEGIC JUDGMENT)
The two segments offering the best combination of size, unmet need, and commercial reachability are: (a) **mainstream private, Tier 1/2 urban, Type 2 newly-initiated** patients (large, price-sensitive but reachable through simplified human/analogue premix offerings and patient-support programs), and (b) **Type 1 diabetes nationally** (smaller but highest-urgency, strong fit for a differentiated access/patient-support program that builds brand trust and government/NGO partnership credibility).
