# Market Growth Scenarios

Three scenarios for the India insulin-treated population and market value over the 5-year horizon, consistent with `10_FINANCIAL_MODEL/`.

## Base Case
- Insulin-treated population CAGR: **~7%**
- Drivers: continued diagnosis-rate improvement, gradual initiation-rate increase, moderate GLP-1 substitution drag (~1–1.5 pts off headline growth)
- Analogue volume mix rises from ~25–30% to ~32–35% of units by year 5
- Market value CAGR (blended price + volume): **~9–11%** (value grows faster than volume due to gradual analogue mix shift, partially offset by ongoing price control)

## Upside Case
- Insulin-treated population CAGR: **~9–10%**
- Drivers: accelerated diagnosis (successful NP-NCD screening scale-up), stronger-than-expected insulin-initiation improvement from education programs, muted GLP-1 substitution (affordability keeps GLP-1 access-limited for most of the Type 2 population over this horizon)
- Analogue mix rises to ~38–42% by year 5 as access/patient-support programs specifically target analogue affordability
- Market value CAGR: **~13–15%**

## Downside Case
- Insulin-treated population CAGR: **~4–5%**
- Drivers: stricter price control extends to more analogue SKUs (compressing value growth even if volume holds), faster-than-expected GLP-1 substitution in the private/insured segment, slower public screening rollout
- Analogue mix stagnates near ~28–30%
- Market value CAGR: **~4–6%**, with visible margin compression across the industry, not just for Novo Nordisk

## Cross-Scenario Constant
In all three scenarios, **volume growth is structurally positive** — diabetes prevalence and the undiagnosed/undertreated pool are large enough that even the downside case does not imply a shrinking patient base, only a slower-than-hoped conversion rate. The strategic risk is concentrated in **value/margin growth**, not patient volume, reinforcing the report's emphasis on portfolio mix and access-program efficiency over pure price defense.
