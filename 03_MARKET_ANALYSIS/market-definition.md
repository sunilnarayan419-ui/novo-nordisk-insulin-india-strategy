# Market Definition

## Product Market
The relevant product market is **insulin therapy for diabetes mellitus in India**, comprising:
- Human insulin (short-acting, NPH, premix) — vials and cartridges
- Analogue insulin (rapid-, long-acting, premix analogue) — vials, cartridges, prefilled pens
- Associated delivery devices (reusable pen devices, needles) where bundled with insulin commercially

Excluded: oral antidiabetic agents and GLP-1 receptor agonists are treated as adjacent/competing therapy classes (substitution risk), not part of the core market being sized.

## Geographic Market
National (India), segmented by:
- **State/region** (proxy for income, healthcare infrastructure, and diabetes prevalence variation)
- **Urban / rural**
- **City tier** (Metro, Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3+)

## Customer/Payer Market
Three distinct "customers" exist for insulin in India, each with different economics:
1. **Patients / households** (predominantly out-of-pocket payers)
2. **Institutions** (government hospitals, AB PM-JAY–empanelled hospitals, state tenders, PMBJP)
3. **Private insurers / corporate health plans** (a minority financing channel for outpatient chronic medicine today, but growing)

## Time Horizon
FY2026–FY2031 (5-year strategic horizon), with a base year set at the most recent full year of available public epidemiological and pricing data.

## Market Boundary Rationale
Insulin is defined as a distinct market from oral agents because it is non-substitutable for Type 1 diabetes and for a meaningful share of advanced Type 2 diabetes — but the boundary is treated as *softening* over the horizon due to GLP-1 receptor agonist encroachment on earlier-stage Type 2 patients (see `market-growth-scenarios.md`).
