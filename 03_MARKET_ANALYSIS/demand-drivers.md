# Demand Drivers

## Structural (Long-Term) Demand Drivers (FACT)
1. Rising and still-underdiagnosed diabetes prevalence (ICMR-INDIAB: 11.4% weighted prevalence, ~101M diagnosed).
2. Large undiagnosed pool (~50%+ of diabetics undiagnosed per multiple public-health sources) — future conversion into diagnosed, then treated, then insulin-eligible populations.
3. Aging population and rising Type 2 duration — beta-cell decline over time mechanically raises the share of patients needing insulin intensification.
4. Urbanization and dietary transition — well-established epidemiological drivers of Type 2 incidence.
5. Government screening expansion (NP-NCD) — increases the diagnosed base, a precursor to treatment and insulin initiation.

## Demand Accelerators (Commercial Levers — CA)
6. Physician education reducing "clinical inertia" in insulin initiation timing.
7. Simplified regimens (premix, once-daily basal analogues) lowering the behavioral barrier to initiation.
8. Patient-support/affordability programs converting price-deferred patients into active users.
9. Improved last-mile distribution and cold chain converting currently-unreachable rural demand into served demand.

## Demand Dampeners (FACT/SA)
10. GLP-1 receptor agonist availability — increasingly used earlier in the Type 2 pathway, delaying or in some patients avoiding insulin initiation; this is a genuine, growing headwind over the 5-year horizon, especially in the premium/private segment.
11. Persistent affordability barriers in low-income/rural segments suppress realized demand well below clinical need (the gap between "need" and "served demand" is itself a driver of latent, not realized, growth).
12. Biosimilar/generic price competition does not reduce patient-level demand but compresses the revenue captured per unit of demand for originator companies.

## Net Assessment
Structural demand drivers dominate over the 5-year horizon; the base-case model (`10_FINANCIAL_MODEL/`) nets these to a **6–8% CAGR in insulin-treated population**, below raw prevalence growth potential due to GLP-1 substitution at the margin, but well above flat.
