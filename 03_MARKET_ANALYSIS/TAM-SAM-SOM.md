# TAM / SAM / SOM

## TAM (Total Addressable Market)
All insulin-treated patients in India, across all payer channels and all insulin categories.
- **Population**: ~12–13 million patients (see `market-sizing.md`)
- **Value basis**: Using a blended average annual cost per patient across the full human-insulin-to-analogue mix — estimated at roughly **₹3,000–15,000/year** depending on regimen and channel (derived from NPPA ceiling prices for human insulin at the low end and retail analogue pen pricing at the high end) — the **TAM is estimated at roughly ₹5,000–12,000 crore (~US$0.6–1.4 billion) annually**, a wide range reflecting the absence of an authoritative single public figure and the wide within-market price dispersion.

## SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)
The segment realistically reachable by a multinational originator like Novo Nordisk given its current registered product range, channel presence, and price-control constraints — i.e., excluding the portion of TAM served exclusively by ultra-low-cost, government-tender-only, hyper-local generic suppliers with which Novo Nordisk does not currently compete.
- **Estimated at roughly 55–65% of TAM**, reflecting Novo Nordisk's presence across both human insulin and analogue segments but limited participation in the most aggressively priced pure-generic government tenders.
- **SAM ≈ ₹2,800–7,800 crore (~US$0.33–0.9 billion)**

## SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market — 5-year horizon)
Novo Nordisk's realistically obtainable share within the SAM, reflecting current competitive position (a top-3 global insulin player, present in India across both human and analogue insulin) and the investment level assumed in the recommended strategy (see `11_STRATEGIC_OPTIONS/`).
- **Current estimated Novo Nordisk India insulin revenue share of SAM: consulting-assumption range of 15–25%** (Novo Nordisk does not disclose India insulin-specific revenue; range is informed by its position as one of the "big three" globally and IQVIA Institute data showing big-three combined LMIC share often in the 60–75% range where they compete, moderated downward for India's unusually high domestic/biosimilar volume share).
- Under the recommended strategy, this report targets **SOM expansion toward 20–28% of SAM by year 5**, driven by segmented pricing (recovering volume in price-sensitive channels) and analogue mix growth in the premium/private channel (see `10_FINANCIAL_MODEL/`).
