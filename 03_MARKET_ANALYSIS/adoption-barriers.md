# Adoption Barriers

## Patient-Level Barriers (FACT/DBI)
1. **Cost** — the dominant barrier for most non-premium patients; NPPA ceiling-price data shows a >15x per-ml price gap between price-controlled human insulin and even price-controlled analogue pens, before accounting for uncontrolled premium analogue pricing.
2. **Injection aversion / stigma** — well-documented in diabetology literature as a driver of initiation delay, particularly the perception that "starting insulin" signals disease severity or personal failure.
3. **Lack of outpatient insurance coverage** — most Indian health insurance (private and AB PM-JAY) is hospitalization-focused; daily self-administered insulin is rarely covered as an outpatient benefit, forcing near-total out-of-pocket exposure for a lifelong chronic cost.
4. **Complexity of regimens** — multiple-daily-injection regimens are harder to sustain without structured education/support, especially in lower-literacy or resource-constrained households.

## Physician-Level Barriers (DBI)
5. **Clinical inertia** — delaying insulin initiation despite guideline-indicated need, a globally documented phenomenon that is not India-specific but is compounded in India by cost-conversation avoidance between physician and patient.
6. **Limited endocrinology specialist density** outside metro areas, meaning general physicians manage most diabetes care but may have less structured insulin-initiation training/support.

## System-Level Barriers (FACT)
7. **Cold-chain and last-mile distribution gaps** in Tier 3+/rural India physically limit availability regardless of price or willingness (see `09_SUPPLY_CHAIN/`).
8. **Fragmented, hospitalization-centric public financing** (AB PM-JAY) that does not structurally fund chronic outpatient insulin use.
9. **Price control uncertainty** — while price control (NPPA/DPCO) helps affordability, its scope (e.g., recent addition of insulin glargine to NLEM) creates commercial planning uncertainty for manufacturers deciding where to invest in access programs vs. price-controlled segments.

## Strategic Implication
No single lever (price cut alone, education alone, or distribution alone) resolves adoption — the barriers are multi-layered, which is the core argument for an ecosystem-style strategic response rather than a single-lever strategy (see `11_STRATEGIC_OPTIONS/option-D-ecosystem-strategy.md`).
