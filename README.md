# 01 — Executive Summary

India presents a complex insulin strategy environment where **clinical need, affordability, market access, competitive dynamics, distribution, healthcare economics, and commercial sustainability intersect**.

This project examines that environment through the lens of an independent strategy consulting engagement focused on insulin.

Rather than treating insulin simply as a pharmaceutical product, the analysis approaches the market as a **multi-stakeholder healthcare system**.

The project asks:

> **What strategic choices could enable a major insulin-focused pharmaceutical company to expand appropriate access and adoption while maintaining a sustainable business model?**

The analysis moves from:

**Problem Definition → Evidence → Market Analysis → Competitive Intelligence → Economics → Modeling → Strategic Options → Recommendation → Implementation**

---

# 02 — The Consulting Question

## The Core Problem

The strategic challenge can be expressed as:

> **How can an insulin manufacturer increase patient access and appropriate adoption in India without creating an economically unsustainable model?**

This requires balancing competing objectives.

| Strategic Dimension | Key Question                                                   |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🧬 Science          | What technological and biological factors matter?              |
| 🏥 Clinical         | What influences treatment initiation and adherence?            |
| 👥 Patient          | What prevents patients from accessing or remaining on therapy? |
| 💰 Affordability    | How does price influence access and behavior?                  |
| 🏛️ Market Access   | What role do payers and public programs play?                  |
| 📊 Competition      | How are competitors positioned?                                |
| 🚚 Operations       | Can products reliably reach patients?                          |
| 💵 Economics        | What happens to revenue, margins and sustainability?           |
| 🎯 Strategy         | Which strategic choices create the strongest overall outcome?  |

# 03 — Why This Is a Difficult Strategy Problem

A simplistic pharmaceutical strategy might focus on:

**Market Size → Market Share → Revenue**

Healthcare strategy is more complicated.

The project therefore considers:

```text
                    PATIENT NEED
                         │
                         ▼
                 DIAGNOSIS / ACCESS
                         │
                         ▼
                  TREATMENT INITIATION
                         │
                         ▼
                    ADHERENCE
                         │
             ┌───────────┴───────────┐
             ▼                       ▼
        AFFORDABILITY           PHYSICIAN BEHAVIOR
             │                       │
             └───────────┬───────────┘
                         ▼
                   MARKET ACCESS
                         │
                         ▼
                  DISTRIBUTION
                         │
                         ▼
                 PRODUCT AVAILABILITY
                         │
                         ▼
                 PATIENT OUTCOMES
                         │
                         ▼
               ECONOMIC SUSTAINABILITY
```

The strategic implication is important:

> **Improving one part of the system does not necessarily improve the overall outcome.**

# 09 — Quantitative Modeling

A core component of the project is the use of **scenario-based quantitative analysis**.

Python models are maintained under:

```text
15_ANALYTICS/python/
```

The analytical workflow is designed around:

```text
INPUTS
  ↓
ASSUMPTIONS
  ↓
MODEL
  ↓
SCENARIOS
  ↓
SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
  ↓
OUTPUTS
  ↓
STRATEGIC INTERPRETATION
```

The purpose is not to manufacture false precision.

Instead, the model makes assumptions transparent and allows decision-makers to ask:

* What happens if adoption changes?
* What happens if pricing changes?
* What happens if market share changes?
* Which assumptions drive the outcome?
* Where are the largest risks?
* Which strategic choices remain robust under uncertainty?

# 10 — Evidence Architecture

A professional strategy recommendation is only as strong as its evidence.

Therefore, the project separates **facts, assumptions, analysis and judgments**.

| Classification            | Definition                                       |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **FACT**                  | Directly supported by an identified source       |
| **DATA-BASED INFERENCE**  | Analytical conclusion derived from data          |
| **CONSULTING ASSUMPTION** | Assumption necessary to perform analysis         |
| **SCENARIO ASSUMPTION**   | Assumption used to construct a scenario          |
| **STRATEGIC JUDGMENT**    | Recommendation based on synthesis and trade-offs |

Priority is given to:

1. Government / regulatory sources
2. Public company filings
3. Annual reports
4. Investor presentations
5. Peer-reviewed literature
6. Epidemiological datasets
7. Reputable healthcare institutions
8. Industry sources
9. Public market evidence

# 24 — Why This Project Exists

I could have created another academic assignment.

Instead, I wanted to work on a problem closer to what a real life-sciences consultant might encounter.

The goal was to combine:

> **Scientific understanding + business reasoning + quantitative analysis + strategic judgment.**

The project is deliberately imperfect.

That is part of the point.

Real consulting involves incomplete information, uncertain assumptions, conflicting stakeholder incentives, imperfect data, and decisions that cannot wait for perfect certainty.

The capability I am trying to develop is therefore not simply:

**"knowing biotechnology."**

It is:

> **Knowing enough science to understand the problem, enough economics to understand the incentives, enough data analysis to quantify uncertainty, enough strategy to evaluate choices, and enough business judgment to recommend an action.**
