# Patient Cost Analysis

## Total Cost of Diabetes Care Components (FACT/DBI)
1. Insulin itself (highly variable by product tier — see `pricing-landscape.md`)
2. Delivery consumables — syringes or pen needles (recurring, often underestimated cost given multiple daily injections)
3. Blood glucose monitoring — strips, glucometer, or CGM sensors (a substantial and recurring cost, frequently the item patients cut first when budget-constrained, which is clinically dangerous as it removes the feedback needed for safe dose titration)
4. Physician visits / follow-up (variable; more frequent for insulin-intensified or poorly controlled patients)
5. Complication-related costs when control fails (hospitalization, dialysis for nephropathy, cardiovascular events, amputation) — these dwarf routine insulin cost when they occur.

## Illustrative Annual Cost Build (CA)
For a Type 2 patient on premix human insulin, illustrative all-in annual cost (insulin + consumables + routine visits) is estimated in the low single-digit thousands of rupees; for a Type 1 patient on an intensive analogue basal-bolus regimen with monitoring, illustrative all-in annual cost can run into the tens of thousands of rupees — a wide range that explains why household-level affordability strain is so heterogeneous even within "insulin-treated patients" as a single category.

## Cost of Non-Adherence (Comparative)
The literature-consistent finding is that even a single DKA hospitalization or a cardiovascular event resulting from poor control typically costs several multiples of a full year of insulin therapy — reinforcing that supporting persistence (see `05_PRODUCT_AND_PORTFOLIO/convenience-and-adherence.md`) is not just a patient-welfare goal but a total-system-cost-minimizing one.

## Strategic Implication
Framing patient-support investment as "reducing the total cost of diabetes," not just "discounting insulin," is both more accurate and a stronger argument for payer/institutional buy-in (see `08_HEALTH_ECONOMICS/economic-value.md`).
