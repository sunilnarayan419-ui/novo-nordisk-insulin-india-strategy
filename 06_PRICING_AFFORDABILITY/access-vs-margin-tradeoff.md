# Access vs. Margin Trade-off

## The Core Tension
The problem statement explicitly asks how to expand access "without compromising long-term economics" — this is a genuine trade-off, not a false dichotomy, and this report does not pretend otherwise.

## Where the Trade-off Is Real
- Direct price reduction on premium analogues in the private channel would expand access marginally but at a high margin cost, because premium-channel demand is relatively price-inelastic (see `price-sensitivity.md`) — meaning the volume gained would not offset the margin given up. **This is a bad trade for Novo Nordisk.**
- Aggressive price-matching against biosimilar/domestic competitors in the institutional/government channel would likely fail to win meaningfully more tender volume (price-only competition favors the lowest-cost manufacturer structurally) while sacrificing margin across the whole channel. **This is also a bad trade.**

## Where the Trade-off Can Be Resolved (Not Just Balanced)
- **Segmented pricing** avoids giving up margin where it isn't needed to drive access (premium channel) while concentrating access investment where it is needed (rural, low-income, Type 1) — this is not a compromise but a genuinely better allocation of the same total margin pool.
- **Patient-support programs funded by premium-tier margin** convert what would otherwise be a blanket price cut (margin-destructive) into a targeted subsidy (margin-preserving), because it reaches only the patients whose access is genuinely price-constrained rather than discounting all patients including those who would have paid full price anyway.
- **Volume growth from access expansion** partially self-funds the trade-off over time: even at lower per-unit margin, a larger total insulin-treated base sustains long-term relevance, physician relationships, and negotiating position that a shrinking premium-only franchise would lose.

## Bottom Line (STRATEGIC JUDGMENT)
Access and margin are only in tension when pricing is undifferentiated. A well-executed segmented architecture converts most of the apparent trade-off into a genuine win-win — the remaining, irreducible trade-off is the **upfront investment cost** of building the segmentation/patient-support infrastructure itself (see `12_IMPLEMENTATION/investment-requirements.md`), which is a real cost but a bounded, one-time-ish one rather than a permanent margin sacrifice.
