# Health Economics Value Framework

## Framework: Total Cost of Care, Not Acquisition Price
This report evaluates insulin's economic value using a **total cost of care** lens: acquisition cost of insulin/devices, plus consumables (monitoring, delivery supplies), plus routine care, plus the *avoided* cost of complications from poor control — rather than acquisition price in isolation.

## Value Drivers
1. **Glycemic control** (HbA1c reduction/maintenance) → reduces long-term microvascular (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and macrovascular (cardiovascular, stroke) complication risk — the primary long-horizon economic value driver.
2. **Hypoglycemia avoidance** → reduces acute-event costs (ER visits, hospitalization) and productivity loss — the primary short-horizon economic value driver, and the dimension on which analogues most clearly outperform human insulin.
3. **Adherence/persistence support** → determines whether the above value is actually realized in practice; a clinically superior product with poor adherence delivers little real-world value.
4. **Device usability** → indirectly supports adherence and reduces dosing-error-related adverse events.

## Framework Application
Each analytical section (`budget-impact.md`, `treatment-cost-model.md`, `economic-value.md`, `clinical-outcome-value.md`) applies this framework to a specific decision question — respectively: population-level budget impact, per-patient cost modeling, headline economic-value synthesis, and clinical-outcome-to-cost linkage.

## Evidentiary Caveat
India-specific, peer-reviewed cost-effectiveness studies comparing analogue vs. human insulin at a population level are limited in the public domain relative to high-income-market literature; this report uses the best available international comparative-effectiveness and cost literature, applied to India-specific price levels, and flags all resulting figures as **consulting assumptions/estimates**, not validated India-specific health-economic study results.
