# Out-of-Pocket Market

## Scale (DBI)
Out-of-pocket payment is the dominant financing mode for insulin in India today, consistent with the broader pattern that outpatient chronic medication is largely uncovered by both government schemes and most private insurance (see `reimbursement-landscape.md`). This makes the out-of-pocket market not a niche segment but the **majority of the addressable insulin market by patient count**.

## Behavioral Consequences of Out-of-Pocket Financing (FACT/DBI)
- Price sensitivity is maximal, and purchase decisions are frequently made month-to-month rather than based on a fixed annual treatment plan, creating volatility in demand and a higher risk of dose rationing during income shocks (seasonal, medical, or employment-related).
- Smaller, more frequent purchases (rather than bulk/annual purchase) are common, which has implications for pack-size strategy (see `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/pricing-options.md`, Option 3) and for retail/pharmacy inventory design.

## Channel Mix Within Out-of-Pocket
- Retail pharmacy (organized chains and independent) remains the dominant dispensing channel.
- E-pharmacy is a growing channel, particularly in urban/Tier 1–2 India, offering potential price transparency and subscription/refill-reminder models that could directly support adherence (see `05_PRODUCT_AND_PORTFOLIO/convenience-and-adherence.md`).
- PMBJP/Jan Aushadhi is a growing, government-backed low-cost alternative within the out-of-pocket market specifically for human insulin and generics.

## Strategic Implication
Because the out-of-pocket market is the largest single segment, market-access strategy cannot rely solely on institutional/insurer engagement — direct-to-patient affordability levers (pack-size options, patient-support enrollment via pharmacy/e-pharmacy partnerships, PMBJP-aligned distribution) are equally, if not more, important for near-term volume impact.
