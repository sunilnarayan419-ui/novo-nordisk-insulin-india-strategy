# Payer & Stakeholder Map

## Stakeholders
| Stakeholder | Role | Primary Interest |
|---|---|---|
| Patients / households | Predominant payer (out-of-pocket) | Affordability, availability, simplicity |
| Government (Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, NHA, NPPA, State Health Depts.) | Regulator, payer (via schemes), procurer (tenders) | Population health outcomes, fiscal sustainability, affordability optics |
| Private hospitals & hospital chains | Provider, institutional purchaser | Clinical outcomes, cost containment, formulary simplicity |
| Private insurers / TPAs | Payer (minority channel for outpatient chronic drugs) | Claims cost containment |
| Physicians (endocrinologists, diabetologists, general physicians) | Prescriber | Clinical efficacy/safety, ease of use, patient adherence |
| Pharmacists / retail & e-pharmacy chains | Distribution/dispensing | Margin, inventory turnover, availability |
| PMBJP / Jan Aushadhi Kendras | Affordable-generic retail channel | Access, price, government-scheme alignment |
| NGOs / patient advocacy groups (e.g., Type 1 diabetes advocacy organizations) | Patient support, policy advocacy | Access equity, especially pediatric Type 1 |
| Domestic & biosimilar manufacturers | Competitors, occasionally coalition partners on access policy | Market share, government-tender relationships |

## Influence–Interest Assessment
- **High influence, high interest**: Government/NPPA (sets price-control policy directly affecting Novo Nordisk's core economics), large hospital networks (formulary gatekeepers), NHA (shapes AB PM-JAY scope).
- **High influence, lower direct interest in Novo Nordisk specifically**: State governments (tender decisions), private insurers (as outpatient drug coverage evolves).
- **Lower influence, high interest**: Patient advocacy groups, particularly for Type 1 diabetes — important reputational/access partners even though they lack direct pricing power.

## Strategic Implication
Market access strategy must engage government and institutional stakeholders proactively (not just physicians and patients) — pricing and access decisions are as much a function of policy relationships and program design as of physician detailing (see `market-access-strategy.md`).
