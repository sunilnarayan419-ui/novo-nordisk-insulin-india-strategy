# Market Access Strategy

## Strategic Objective
Expand the *effectively reachable* market (patients who can both access and afford appropriate insulin) faster than the underlying diagnosed-population growth rate, by systematically addressing the structural gaps identified across `government-programs.md`, `institutional-procurement.md`, `reimbursement-landscape.md`, `private-insurance.md`, and `out-of-pocket-market.md`.

## Four-Pillar Market Access Plan
1. **Government/PMBJP channel partnership** — formalize a distribution and cold-chain-support partnership to ensure reliable human-insulin availability through Jan Aushadhi Kendras, particularly in Tier 2/3 towns; supports both access goals and government-relationship equity.
2. **Institutional volume contracts** — pursue multi-year, volume-committed pricing agreements with private hospital networks and PM-JAY–empanelled hospitals (see `institutional-procurement.md`), rather than competing on pure lowest-price state tenders.
3. **Insurer/TPA pilot partnerships** — pilot outpatient insulin/monitoring benefit programs with 1–2 large corporate insurers or TPAs, framed around complication-cost avoidance (see `private-insurance.md`).
4. **Direct patient-support infrastructure** — co-pay assistance and enrollment support for defined high-need segments (Type 1 diabetes nationally; documented low-income Type 2 patients), distributed via pharmacy/e-pharmacy and NGO partnerships.

## Sequencing
Pillars 1 and 4 are near-term (Year 1) priorities given lower organizational complexity and direct alignment with the "expand access" mandate; Pillars 2 and 3 are Year 1–2 priorities requiring more institutional negotiation lead time; policy advocacy for expanded outpatient reimbursement (see `reimbursement-landscape.md`) is a Year 2–5 background workstream with uncertain but potentially large long-term payoff.

## Success Metrics
See `12_IMPLEMENTATION/KPI-framework.md` for the full KPI set; headline market-access KPIs include number of PMBJP-aligned distribution points reliably stocked, number of patients enrolled in patient-support programs, and number of institutional volume contracts signed.
