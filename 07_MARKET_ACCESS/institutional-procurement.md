# Institutional Procurement

## Procurement Channels (FACT/DBI)
1. **State government tenders** — bulk procurement for government hospitals/PHCs, typically won on lowest-price-compliant-bid basis, favoring domestic/biosimilar manufacturers with lower cost structures.
2. **Central government procurement** (e.g., via central procurement agencies for national programs) — similar dynamics to state tenders but larger scale.
3. **AB PM-JAY–empanelled private hospital procurement** — hospitals procure directly (not through a centralized formulary), giving branded/analogue manufacturers more relationship-based access than in pure government tenders, though price sensitivity remains high given PM-JAY's package-rate reimbursement structure (hospitals are reimbursed a fixed package rate, incentivizing lower-cost drug procurement within that package).
4. **Private hospital chains (non-PM-JAY)** — greater formulary flexibility, more receptive to premium/analogue products, especially where hospital-employed endocrinologists influence formulary decisions.

## Novo Nordisk's Competitive Position in Institutional Procurement (STRATEGIC JUDGMENT)
Weakest in pure state-tender/lowest-price procurement (structurally disadvantaged versus domestic/biosimilar manufacturers), strongest in private hospital chains where clinical relationships and device quality carry more weight than headline price.

## Recommended Institutional Strategy
- Do not attempt to win pure lowest-price state tenders on price alone; instead, pursue **volume-committed contracts with private hospital networks and select PM-JAY–empanelled hospitals** (Option 5 in `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/pricing-options.md`) where relationship and clinical differentiation are viable competitive levers.
- Selectively participate in state tenders for human insulin where cost structure allows competitive bidding, primarily to maintain government relationships and channel presence rather than as a primary margin source.
