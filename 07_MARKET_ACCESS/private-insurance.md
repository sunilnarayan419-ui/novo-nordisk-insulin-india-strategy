# Private Insurance

## Market Context (FACT/DBI)
- Private health insurance penetration in India remains a minority of the population relative to public-scheme coverage (AB PM-JAY) and out-of-pocket payment, and where private insurance exists, it is predominantly hospitalization-indemnity in structure rather than outpatient/pharmacy-benefit in structure.
- Corporate/group health insurance (employer-sponsored) is more likely than individual retail policies to include limited OPD/outpatient benefits, making the salaried, formally-employed population the most likely near-term beneficiary of any insurance-financed insulin access.

## Implication for Insulin
Private insurance today plays a **limited direct role** in financing routine insulin therapy — its relevance is concentrated in financing **hospitalization for diabetes complications** (which insurers have a direct cost incentive to prevent, creating a potential value-based-partnership opportunity) rather than in financing day-to-day insulin supply.

## Opportunity: Insurer Partnership on Prevention/Complication-Avoidance (STRATEGIC JUDGMENT)
Because insurers bear the cost of complications (DKA admission, cardiovascular events, amputation, dialysis) but not routine outpatient insulin cost, there is a genuine shared-interest opportunity: an insurer that subsidizes or reimburses outpatient insulin/monitoring for at-risk enrollees could reduce its own downstream claims cost. This is the underlying logic that has supported value-based-care and disease-management partnership models in other markets, and represents a longer-horizon, partnership-based market-access lever for Novo Nordisk in India (see `08_HEALTH_ECONOMICS/budget-impact.md` for the underlying cost-avoidance logic).

## Near-Term Recommendation
Pilot a small-scale partnership with one or two large corporate/group health insurers or TPAs to test an outpatient insulin/monitoring benefit tied to demonstrated complication-cost avoidance, rather than assuming near-term insurance-financed volume at scale.
