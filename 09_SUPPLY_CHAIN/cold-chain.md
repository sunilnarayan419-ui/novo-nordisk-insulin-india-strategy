# Cold Chain

## Requirement (FACT)
Insulin must be maintained at **2–8°C** from manufacturing through to near point-of-use; product exposed to freezing or to sustained heat above the labeled threshold loses potency and should not be used, even if it appears visually normal.

## India-Specific Cold-Chain Risk Factors (FACT/DBI)
- India's climate includes prolonged high-ambient-temperature periods across large parts of the country, raising cold-chain-breach risk at every unrefrigerated transit or storage point.
- Rural and Tier 3+ retail/dispensing points frequently have inconsistent or absent reliable refrigeration and inconsistent electricity supply, a well-documented general challenge for temperature-sensitive pharmaceuticals and vaccines in India (the same infrastructure gap that has shaped India's vaccine cold-chain investment under its Universal Immunization Programme, from which insulin distribution can draw operational lessons).
- General industry/LMIC literature on temperature-sensitive biologics cites cold-chain-related wastage in the broad range of roughly a quarter to a third of product in some developing-region contexts (industry analysis, general LMIC insulin commentary) — **no India-insulin-specific public wastage figure was identified in this research**, so this is used as a directional consulting-assumption range, not a validated India figure (see `00_PROJECT_CHARTER/assumptions-register.md`, A6).

## Where Breaches Are Most Likely (CA)
1. Last-mile transport (stockist → rural pharmacy/Jan Aushadhi Kendra) via non-refrigerated vehicles
2. Retail-point storage without reliable refrigeration or backup power
3. Extended transit during peak summer months in non-cold-chain-certified logistics partners

*See diagram: `../16_EXHIBITS/supply-chain/cold-chain-risk-map.svg`.*

## Mitigation Levers
1. Partner with existing vaccine/biologics cold-chain logistics infrastructure and providers rather than building parallel infrastructure from scratch
2. Solar-powered or battery-backed refrigeration units for rural retail/PMBJP outlets, potentially co-invested with government PMBJP infrastructure programs
3. Cold-chain indicator technology (temperature-logging labels) on distribution units to identify and quarantine breached stock before it reaches patients
4. Prioritize cold-chain investment in the highest-opportunity Tier 2/3 geographies identified in `03_MARKET_ANALYSIS/geographic-opportunity.md`, rather than attempting uniform national upgrade simultaneously

## Strategic Implication
Cold-chain investment is a **prerequisite**, not a nice-to-have, for the access expansion this engagement recommends in Tier 3+/rural geographies — sequencing this investment ahead of demand-generation spend in those geographies avoids wasting commercial investment on markets that cannot yet reliably receive product (see `12_IMPLEMENTATION/implementation-roadmap.md`).
