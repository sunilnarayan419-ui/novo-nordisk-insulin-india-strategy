# Inventory Economics

## Key Trade-offs
1. **Shelf-life vs. cold-chain cost**: insulin has a defined shelf life under refrigeration; holding excess inventory to avoid stock-outs increases cold-chain-storage cost and spoilage/write-off risk if demand forecasts are wrong.
2. **Pack size vs. inventory turnover**: smaller pack sizes (aligned with the affordability pack-size strategy in `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/pricing-options.md`) increase transaction frequency and distribution/handling cost per unit of drug delivered, but reduce upfront patient cash burden and retailer inventory-value-at-risk.
3. **Centralized vs. distributed cold storage**: centralizing cold storage reduces fixed-infrastructure cost but increases last-mile cold-chain distance/risk; a more distributed network of smaller certified cold-storage points reduces last-mile risk but raises total infrastructure investment.

## Illustrative Economics (CA)
A stock-out or spoilage event on institutional/government-tender volume is typically lower-margin-per-unit-lost than the same event on private-retail/premium-analogue volume — meaning inventory-buffer investment should be risk-weighted toward protecting the **higher-margin private/institutional-contract channels** first, while using lower-cost mitigation (PMBJP partnership, VMI) for higher-volume/lower-margin government-channel human insulin.

## Working Capital Consideration
Extending buffer-stock financing support to smaller rural retailers (per `availability-barriers.md`) has a working-capital cost for Novo Nordisk (or its distribution partners) that should be explicitly modeled as part of the market-access investment case, not treated as a free operational fix (see `10_FINANCIAL_MODEL/` and `12_IMPLEMENTATION/investment-requirements.md`).

## Strategic Implication
Inventory and cold-chain investment decisions should be explicitly prioritized by **channel margin and access-strategic-value**, not applied uniformly — reinforcing the segmented approach that runs throughout this report's pricing, portfolio, and access recommendations.
