# Distribution Network

## Current Structure (FACT/DBI)
Pharmaceutical distribution in India typically flows: manufacturer → carrying and forwarding (C&F) agent/regional depot → wholesaler/stockist → retail pharmacy or hospital pharmacy — a multi-tier structure common across the industry, not insulin-specific, but with the added cold-chain requirement layered on top for insulin specifically.

## Channel-Specific Distribution Paths
| Channel | Distribution Path | Cold-Chain Robustness |
|---|---|---|
| Metro/Tier 1 organized retail & hospital pharmacy | Manufacturer → regional CFA → organized wholesaler → pharmacy | High — established cold-chain logistics providers, reliable infrastructure |
| Government tender supply | Manufacturer → direct/state medical store depot → PHC/govt hospital | Variable — depends on state-level cold-chain investment |
| PMBJP / Jan Aushadhi | Manufacturer/BPPI-empanelled supplier → PMBJP central/regional warehousing → Kendra | Improving but historically more oriented to non-cold-chain generics; insulin-specific cold-chain buildout is comparatively newer |
| Rural/Tier 3+ independent pharmacy | Manufacturer → stockist → sub-stockist → retail | Lowest reliability — longest, most fragmented last-mile |
| E-pharmacy | Manufacturer/distributor → e-pharmacy fulfillment center → direct-to-patient courier | Emerging — cold-chain-compliant last-mile courier is a differentiator among e-pharmacy players, still maturing |

*See diagram: `../16_EXHIBITS/supply-chain/insulin-distribution-flow.svg`.*

## Distribution Gaps and Opportunities
- **E-pharmacy with cold-chain-compliant delivery** is an underexploited channel for reaching Tier 2 patients with reliable, direct, temperature-controlled delivery, bypassing some last-mile fragility of traditional rural retail.
- **PMBJP warehousing partnership** is the most scalable lever for improving rural human-insulin distribution reliability, given PMBJP's existing 18,000+ outlet network and continued government investment.

## Strategic Implication
Rather than building entirely new distribution infrastructure, Novo Nordisk's most capital-efficient path is to **layer targeted cold-chain investment and quality partnerships onto existing high-potential channels** (PMBJP, established cold-chain logistics providers, e-pharmacy) rather than attempting to build a parallel distribution network.
