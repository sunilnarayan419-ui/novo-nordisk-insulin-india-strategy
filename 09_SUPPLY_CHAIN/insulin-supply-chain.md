# Insulin Supply Chain Overview

## End-to-End Chain (FACT — general biologics/insulin supply chain structure)
1. **API manufacturing** — recombinant fermentation (E. coli/yeast host), purification, crystallization (see `02_INDUSTRY_LANDSCAPE/insulin-technology.md`)
2. **Formulation & fill-finish** — formulation into vials/cartridges, aseptic fill-finish (a specialized, capital-intensive step)
3. **Cold-chain primary distribution** — 2–8°C controlled transport from manufacturing site to national/regional distribution centers
4. **Secondary distribution** — regional DC to wholesalers/stockists, maintaining cold chain
5. **Tertiary/last-mile distribution** — stockist to pharmacy/hospital/Jan Aushadhi Kendra, the most cold-chain-vulnerable step in India given infrastructure variability
6. **Point of dispensing** — pharmacy/hospital storage (refrigeration required) and patient counseling on subsequent home storage
7. **Patient home storage & use** — patients are advised to refrigerate unopened insulin (2–8°C) and can typically store an in-use vial/pen at room temperature (below ~30°C) for a limited period (per standard product labeling) — a step entirely outside manufacturer/distributor control but critical to product integrity.

*See diagram: `../16_EXHIBITS/supply-chain/insulin-distribution-flow.svg`.*

## Why Insulin's Supply Chain Is Distinctively Difficult
Unlike most small-molecule tablets/capsules stocked at scale through PMBJP and general retail pharmacy, insulin is a **temperature-sensitive biologic** requiring unbroken cold chain from fermentation through to (near) point of use — this is the central operational fact shaping every downstream analysis in this section.

## Strategic Implication
Supply-chain resilience, not just manufacturing capacity, materially determines *realized* market size in Tier 3+/rural India — addressed in `cold-chain.md`, `distribution-network.md`, and `availability-barriers.md`.
