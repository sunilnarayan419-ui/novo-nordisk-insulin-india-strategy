# Option A — Premium / Innovation-Led Strategy

## Description
Concentrate investment in the newest-generation analogue insulins and premium devices, targeting the premium private/insured and complex-need segments; defend price and margin; treat human insulin and price-sensitive channels as low-priority/legacy.

## Evaluation
| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Patient impact | Low–moderate — reaches only the ~10–15% premium-channel population well |
| Clinical value | High for the patients reached |
| Market potential | Limited — premium channel is the smallest segment (`03_MARKET_ANALYSIS/patient-segmentation.md`) |
| Revenue potential | Moderate near-term, declining long-term as this segment saturates and biosimilar/GLP-1 pressure grows |
| Margin implications | High near-term margin, but on a shrinking addressable base |
| Investment requirements | Moderate (specialist detailing, device marketing) |
| Competitive response | Invites direct biosimilar/domestic competition for the ceded majority market, strengthening rivals long-term |
| Execution complexity | Low — closest to a continuation of current specialist-led commercial motion |
| Regulatory/access risk | High — a "premium-only" positioning is politically exposed to further price-control action and public criticism given the access mandate explicit in the problem statement |
| Strategic sustainability | Low — cedes long-term relevance and physician-relationship breadth to competitors |

## Verdict
**Not recommended as a standalone strategy.** Scores well on near-term margin but poorly on patient impact, market potential, and sustainability — directly conflicts with the mandate to expand access. Retained as the "premium tier" component within the recommended segmented strategy (Option C/D), not as the sole strategy.
