# Option B — Access and Affordability-Led Strategy

## Description
Prioritize volume and access: aggressive pricing across the portfolio (including analogues), broad institutional/government-tender participation, wide affordability-program rollout, minimal differentiation on premium positioning.

## Evaluation
| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Patient impact | High — directly targets the majority price-sensitive population |
| Clinical value | Moderate — depends on which products are pushed at low price; risk of over-indexing on human insulin at the expense of appropriate analogue use for high-risk patients |
| Market potential | High volume potential |
| Revenue potential | Moderate — volume growth, but at compressed unit price |
| Margin implications | **High risk** — competing on price against India-based biosimilar/domestic manufacturers with a structurally lower cost base is a difficult fight to win sustainably (see `04_COMPETITIVE_INTELLIGENCE/competitor-response-scenarios.md`) |
| Investment requirements | High (price investment + program infrastructure) with uncertain payback |
| Competitive response | Domestic/biosimilar players can match or undercut further, given their cost advantage — risk of a margin race-to-the-bottom |
| Execution complexity | Moderate |
| Regulatory/access risk | Low — well-aligned with government access priorities, may reduce further adverse price-control risk |
| Strategic sustainability | Low–moderate — financially fragile if competitors respond aggressively on price |

## Verdict
**Not recommended as a standalone strategy.** Best access/patient-impact score of the four options, but financially fragile — a pure price-led fight against lower-cost domestic manufacturing is not a sustainable path to defend "long-term economics" as the problem statement requires. Its access-oriented instincts are retained within Option C/D, but implemented through targeted programs rather than blanket price competition.
