# Option C — Segmented Portfolio + Differentiated Access Strategy

## Description
Deploy a deliberately segmented "good-better-best" portfolio and pricing architecture (per `05_PRODUCT_AND_PORTFOLIO/portfolio-strategy.md` and `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/pricing-options.md`): premium analogues priced and marketed for the premium/complex-need segment; mainstream analogue/premix products priced for Tier 1/2 mainstream private patients; human insulin defended in institutional/rural channels; combined with targeted (not blanket) patient-support programs for the highest-need patients (Type 1, documented low-income).

## Evaluation
| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Patient impact | High — reaches all segments with an appropriately matched offer |
| Clinical value | High — analogue promotion is targeted to patients with the strongest clinical case (see `08_HEALTH_ECONOMICS/economic-value.md`) |
| Market potential | High — captures value across the full TAM, not just one slice |
| Revenue potential | Strong — premium-channel margin funds access investment without being diluted across the whole portfolio |
| Margin implications | Favorable — avoids unnecessary margin give-up in inelastic premium segment (see `06_PRICING_AFFORDABILITY/access-vs-margin-tradeoff.md`) |
| Investment requirements | Moderate — primarily commercial/program infrastructure, not R&D |
| Competitive response | More resilient — competes on the right basis in each channel rather than a single, exploitable positioning |
| Execution complexity | Moderate–high — requires strong internal pricing/channel discipline to avoid leakage between tiers |
| Regulatory/access risk | Low–moderate — visible access investment provides some insulation against further adverse price-control action |
| Strategic sustainability | High |

## Verdict
**Strongest standalone option** on the weighted scorecard (see `strategic-scorecard.md`). Forms the foundation of the final recommendation.
